# E0: teacher and signal validity

Pilot, dev seed 7, CPU on GitHub Actions, no training. Raw verdicts in `results/e0_run1/`. Sample sizes are small (n = 36 to 60 per criterion), so differences of a few points mean little. Qwen2.5-3B was not run, because a smaller model passed.

## H0: can the model use context? (in-context accuracy)

| Criterion | Threshold | 0.5B base | 0.5B Instruct | 1.5B base | 1.5B Instruct |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct | 0.90 | **0.97** | **0.97** | **1.00** | **0.98** |
| Reverse | 0.70 | **0.95** | **0.98** | **1.00** | **1.00** |
| Two-hop, both facts in context | 0.60 | **0.85** | 0.20 (fail) | **1.00** | 0.58 (fail) |
| Follows contradicting context | 0.70 | **0.83** | **0.75** | **0.92** | 0.59 (fail) |

Rule: smallest model passing all four, so **base 0.5B**. Base 1.5B is a clearly stronger teacher and the natural scale check (E6).

Literature warned small teachers may be unusable (T1 §7B); on simple factual lookup, these base models aren't. That says nothing about harder material.

**Instruct is the worse teacher at both sizes, and on the same two criteria.** Falling contradiction-following with instruction tuning is what context-parametric inversion (C9) reports, in larger models. Two-hop has no such precedent that I know of. Chat-template refusals may be part of it; that is unverified. At 0.5B contradiction still passes, so the pattern isn't uniform.

## H1: does the signal mean anything?

| Criterion | Threshold | 0.5B base | 0.5B Instruct | 1.5B base | 1.5B Instruct |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Own vs. unrelated episode, E0 settings | 0.85 | 0.836 | 0.663 | 0.831 | 0.833 |
| Own vs. unrelated, deployed settings | 0.85 | 0.573 | 0.580 | 0.683 | 0.780 |
| CHD offset vs. raw KL, E0 settings | CHD lower | 1.08 vs. 1.26 | 0.98 vs. 1.09 | 1.39 vs. 1.42 | 1.04 vs. 1.33 |
| CHD offset vs. raw KL, deployed | CHD lower | 1.23 vs. 1.18 (fail) | 1.05 vs. 1.23 | 1.53 vs. 1.15 (fail) | 1.10 vs. 1.29 |
| Known vs. fictional, within type | 0.75 | **0.997** | **0.995** | **0.959** | **0.983** |

**Pre-registered consequence:** remove the control layer. No model passes either episode test, at any setting. Applied in PREREG Amendment 2.

## Reading

1. **My main bet failed at its first test, on all four models.** Controlling with a distractor barely reduced the offset, so the noise isn't format, as I'd argued in draft 1. It's content: an unknown answer is high-entropy, and any context pushes it around.
2. **Size helps the deployed test but doesn't rescue it** (0.57 to 0.68 on base, 0.58 to 0.78 on Instruct). Whether larger models would pass is untested.
3. **Part of the deployed failure may be the test's own fault.** It labels every generated question positive, though many aren't answered by the episode. Whether that explains the gap is unknown; E0b separates them.
4. **Known vs. fictional at 0.96 to 0.997 is the surprise, and it deserves suspicion.** It compares real entities with invented names, so name familiarity is a confound. It needs the within-fact test (H8). It held on all four models.
5. If H8 holds, the framework's value shifts from *deciding what to teach* to *knowing what was learned*: an oracle-free audit, which the literature search did not find. That is a hypothesis, not a result.

## Practical

- Parse rate on generated questions: 0.90 to 1.00. Quality is visibly poor at 0.5B; many are irrelevant or invented.
- Cost on 4-vCPU CPU: 0.5B base 51 min, 0.5B Instruct 71 min, 1.5B base 205 min, 1.5B Instruct 321 min. Name-familiarity check: no hits on any model, nothing replaced.
- All generation ran without fp16. GPU paths remain unexercised.
